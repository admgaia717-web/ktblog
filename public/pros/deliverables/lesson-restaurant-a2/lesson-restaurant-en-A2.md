# Ordering Food at a Restaurant

**Level:** CEFR A2
**Audience:** Adult learners of English (first language: Japanese)
**Lesson time:** 45–50 minutes

---

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:

1. Order food and drinks politely at a restaurant.
2. Ask simple questions about the menu.
3. Make a special request (for example: "No onions, please.").

---

## Warm-up

Answer with a partner or in a small group.

1. Do you like eating out? What is your favorite kind of restaurant?
2. When you go to an Italian restaurant, what do you usually order?
3. Do you know any English phrases for ordering food? Share them with your group.

## Conversation: Friday Night Dinner

**Scene:** Friday evening. Yui is at a small Italian restaurant. She sits down and opens the menu. A server comes to her table.

**Part 1 — Ordering**

**Server:** Good evening! Are you ready to order?

**Yui:** Yes, but I have a question first. What's today's soup?

**Server:** It's tomato soup. It's very popular.

**Yui:** Great. I'd like a bowl of soup and a green salad, please.

**Server:** Sure. Would you like anything to drink?

**Yui:** Can I have an iced tea, please?

**Server:** Of course. And what would you like for your main dish?

**Yui:** The grilled chicken, please.

**Server:** Good choice. Our chicken is a little spicy. Is that OK?

**Yui:** Oh. Then mild, please. And no onions — I don't eat onions.

**Server:** No problem. So that's one tomato soup, one green salad, one mild grilled chicken, and one iced tea.

**Yui:** That's right. Thank you!

**Server:** You're welcome. Your food will be ready in about fifteen minutes.

**Part 2 — The Food Arrives**

*(Fifteen minutes later. The server comes back with the food.)*

**Server:** Here you are. One tomato soup and one green salad.

**Yui:** Thank you. Hmm, this soup smells great!

**Server:** I'm happy to hear that. How is everything?

**Yui:** Very good, thank you.

**Server:** Would you like a dessert? We have ice cream.

**Yui:** No, thank you. I'm full. Can I have the bill, please?

**Server:** Of course. Please pay at the front when you are ready.

**Yui:** Thank you very much. The chicken was excellent!

**Server:** We're glad you enjoyed it. Please come again!

## Grammar Point: "Would you like...?" and Polite Ordering

At a restaurant, "I want ~" sounds too direct. Use these polite patterns instead.

### The three patterns

| Pattern | Use | Example |
|---------|-----|---------|
| **Would you like ~?** | Offer something to the customer | Would you like anything to drink? |
| **I'd like ~, please.** | Say your order politely (= I would like ~) | I'd like a bowl of soup, please. |
| **Can I have ~, please?** | Ask for something politely | Can I have an iced tea, please? |

### More examples

- Would you like a dessert?
- I'd like the grilled chicken, please.
- Can I have some water, please?

### Common mistakes

| ❌ Not natural | ✅ Natural | Why |
|----------------|-----------|-----|
| I want a soup. | I'd like a bowl of soup, please. | Use "I'd like," not "I want." In English you do not say "a soup" — say "a bowl of soup." |
| Give me a menu. | Can I have a menu, please? | "Give me" sounds like an order. Use "Can I have ~?" |
| You like a drink? | Would you like a drink? | The polite question needs "Would" — all three words: "Would you like...?" |

## Answers & Explanations

**Exercise A**

1. **Would** — "Would you like...?" is the polite offer pattern. All three words are needed.
2. **like** — "I would like" = "I'd like." This is the polite way to order.
3. **have** — "Can I have ~, please?" is a common, polite request at a restaurant.

**Exercise B**

4. **I'd like the grilled chicken, please.**
   Explanation: The pattern is "I'd like" + menu item + "please." The comma comes before "please."

**Exercise C**

5. **Model answer:** I'd like a glass of water, please. And can I have the fish? No onions, please — I don't eat onions.
   Explanation: Students' answers can be different. Check two points: (1) a polite form ("I'd like" or "Can I have"), and (2) "please."

**Check-up (Grammar mini practice)**

1. I'd like an iced tea, please. (or: Can I have an iced tea, please?)
2. Can I have the menu, please?

## Culture Corner: Eating Out in the US and the UK

- In the **US**, a tip for the server is normal: about 15–20% of the bill. You leave it on the table or add it when you pay.
- In the **UK**, some restaurants add a "service charge" (about 12.5%) to the bill. If it is already there, you do not need more.
- In both countries, water is often free — just ask: "Can I have some water, please?"
- Your server may ask, "How is everything?" — a short answer is fine: "Great, thank you!"

## Notes for Teachers

**Time plan (45–50 minutes)**

- Warm-up: 5 min (pairs)
- Conversation: 10 min (read twice — once by the teacher, once in pairs)
- Vocabulary: 10 min (check meanings, then cover the table and quiz 3–4 words)
- Grammar: 10 min (drill the three patterns with substitution)
- Practice: 8 min (Exercises A–C)
- Wrap-up role-play: 5 min (pairs: one student is the server, one is the customer; then switch)

**Notes**

- Level check: all vocabulary in this lesson is A2. Do not add extra grammar (no relative clauses, no conditionals).
- "Soup" has no plural in everyday English. Teach "a bowl of soup" as one phrase — do not explain countable/uncountable rules in detail at this level.
- Common A2 learner error: "You like a drink?" (missing "Would"). Drill the full question chorally.
- Exercise C is free writing. Accept any reasonable order; check politeness forms and "please."
- Extension idea: students role-play ordering at their own favorite restaurant type (Japanese, Italian, fast food).
